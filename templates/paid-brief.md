# Paid Creative Brief

Boosting a post is not an advertising strategy. Nothing spends until all seven
boxes are filled.

| Field | This campaign |
| --- | --- |
| **Audience** | Who exactly? Geography, radius, parent age, interests, lookalike source. |
| **Funnel objective** | awareness / interest / visit / inquiry / application / enrollment -- pick one |
| **Psychological objective** | What must they believe after seeing it that they did not believe before? |
| **Hook** | First 1-3 seconds, on-screen. Which structure, and what proves it honest? |
| **Proposition** | The specific thing being offered. Not "learn more". |
| **Proof** | The evidence in the creative. A name, a number, a destination, a face. |
| **Call to action** | One. Where does it land, and is the landing page UTM-tagged? |
| **Measurement** | The single number that decides whether this ran again. |
| **Budget & window** | Amount, dates, and the funnel phase it belongs to. |
| **Organic source** | Which organic winner is this derived from, and at what index? |

## Rules

- Derived from an organic winner, but **rebuilt**: its own ending, its own CTA,
  its own measurement. The organic ending is almost never the right paid ending.
- Consent confirmed for every student in frame. No exceptions, ever.
- Every statistic verified and sourced before the creative is built.
- Sixth and seventh graders are not a paid conversion audience. They are an
  awareness audience; asking them to apply breaks the strategy.
- Seasonal: heaviest in the visit window (mid-October to mid-December) and the
  yield window (February to mid-April). Near zero in June.
- If it cannot be tied to a downstream admissions behavior, it is a brand buy --
  call it that honestly and budget it as one.
