# 1. One-page brief

## Who we are talking to

A parent in Metro Detroit with a 7th or 8th grader at home, who is deciding — this month, mostly in silence, mostly at night — whether Orchard Lake St. Mary's is the next school.

They are not shopping for adulthood. They are shopping for the next two to six years. Their first door is core academics, then independent thinking. Character, citizenship and faith matter to them and are not the door they knock on first. They will not ask whether a 13-year-old is job-ready, and if we answer that question they will assume we did not hear the one they asked.

What actually keeps them up:

- The academic jump. Too much work. Falling behind and finding out late.
- Friends. Belonging. Being unknown in a building full of older kids.
- Bullying, stress, phones, the peer world their child is about to enter without them.
- Six teachers instead of one. Lockers. Getting organizationally lost.
- Safety, and whether any adult in that building will know their child's name.
- The whole thing compressed: *is this the right next world?*

EdChoice's 2026 finding that bullying and stress/anxiety are top reasons families switch schools is not a side note for this audience. In these grades it is hotter than anything about adulting, and Movement 3 of this campaign is built on it.

**Secondary audience:** the 7th and 8th graders themselves, on TikTok and in the student-facing cuts. They have a vote. They do not sign the contract. Nothing in this campaign is written as if they do.

**Explicitly not the audience for these 30 days:** high-school seniors, boarding independence, internships, first jobs, alumni careers. Those are the far next. They appear at most once per movement, never as a hero, never as the answer to "what happens next."

## What "next" means

Across the campaign, "next" means **high school in this building** — harder work on purpose, new peers, more of their own time, and sitting in the same educational room as students five years older.

| | "Next" means |
|---|---|
| **Day 1 — Belonging + Place** | Next is a place you would want to be on an ordinary Monday. Not an event. A Monday. |
| **Day 2 — The Work + Both Divisions** | Next is work that is genuinely harder, done in the same room as the oldest students in the school, at a specific time on a clock. |
| **Day 3 — Judgment + Visit** | Next is being asked a real question at thirteen, in front of people, with an adult who does not resolve it for you — and a parent who can come stand in the back of that room. |
| **Movement 2 (Days 8–14) — Known** | Next is bigger, and still knows your child's name. Here are the numbers and the adults. |
| **Movement 3 (Days 15–21) — Pressure** | Next includes the hard week: the phone, the failed quiz, the lunch table, the kid who is being messed with. Here is the mechanism, not the promise. |
| **Movement 4 (Days 22–30) — The Door** | Next is 8th grade turning into 9th grade in this same building — and a date you can put on a calendar. |

**Horizon line** — *High school should prepare you for all of it. College is the floor.* — is used **three times in thirty days**: Day 3, Day 21, Day 30. Not on Day 1. Never twice in a week. It is a floor, not a chorus.

## What we will not say

- No new tagline. Not "Think. Act. Become." Not "Your Choice. Your World." Not a fifth franchise smuggled in as a hashtag.
- No "We are proud to…" No "amazing opportunity." No "excellent," "vibrant," "transformative," "world-class," "unparalleled," "second to none."
- No adjective in the first five seconds of any video.
- No posed lineup smile as the hero image. No students looking into the lens on cue.
- No employment skills as the lead. No AI-as-the-brand. No alumni first jobs.
- No "we are launching a campaign." Nobody announces a campaign to the people it is aimed at.
- No boarding-independence story unless the family in frame is a boarding prospect. "Nobody reminds you at 6:15 in the dorm" is a senior's line and a boarder's line, and it answers a question a 7th-grade parent did not ask.
- No promise that bullying does not happen here. We show the mechanism — a named adult, a place to go, a person who notices — and we let the parent draw the conclusion.
- No crucifix-plus-quote as the faith post. Faith shows up as the operating system for how people here treat each other and make a decision, or it does not show up.
- No moral voiceover. The school asks. A student or an adult does something. We do not narrate what it meant.

## The single association we want after 72 hours

> **"That's the next school — the one where the work gets real and my kid is in the room with everybody else. And I can go see it on a [Tuesday]."**

Four words if it has to compress: **the next school, in the room.**

At Day 30, the same association, held for a month and converted: the parent has a date on their calendar.

**We have not won if** the association after 72 hours is "pretty campus," "good football," "nice Catholic school," or "prepares kids for the real world." The last one especially — it means we posted the far next and they heard a brochure.

## One structural decision, stated because it looks like a mistake

**Day 1 does not ask the campaign question.** The rules say belonging posts carry no campaign question and no resolution line, and Day 1's hero is a belonging post. So Day 1 is a Monday with the sound left in and no line at the end. The question arrives Day 2 and lands Day 3.

This is deliberate. A parent who has not yet wanted the place will not care what happens next in it. Day 1 buys the right to ask.

### Kill test, run live on the first draft

**First draft, rejected:**

## Three banned captions that look tempting

These are not straw men. Each one is something a good writer would produce on a Thursday afternoon, and each one quietly loses this audience.

### Banned #1
> ❌ *"Where a 13-year-old learns to think for themselves."*

**Why it's tempting:** it is short, it is on-strategy, it names the age, and independent thinking is the second thing K–8 parents say they want.

**Why it fails:** the **any-school test**, absolutely. Every school in the comparison set would sign that sentence today, and three of them have it on a banner. It is also a moral voiceover — it tells the parent what the video meant instead of letting the eleven seconds of silence mean it. And it is a **new tagline**, which is banned outright. The fix is not a better abstraction; the fix is Example D's caption, which contains no abstraction at all and makes the same claim by reporting what two kids said.

### Banned #2
> ❌ *"College is the floor."* — used alone, as the caption on the Day 1 belonging Reel.

**Why it's tempting:** it is the sanctioned horizon line. It is confident, it is short, and it sounds like a school that isn't insecure.

**Why it fails:** it is the horizon line doing a job it was not built for. Day 1 is a belonging post — **no campaign question and no resolution line** — and this is the most resolution-shaped line in the campaign. It also answers a question the Day 1 parent has not asked yet: they are still deciding whether their child would want to walk in that door on a Monday, and we have just changed the subject to outcomes. Used three times in thirty days, on Days 3, 21 and 30, the line is a floor. Used on Day 1, it is a brag over a picture of a lake.

### Banned #3
> ❌ *"Formation, [10:05]. Every student. Every day."*

**Why it's tempting:** it is specific, it has a clock in it, it passes the adjective test cleanly, and it names the most distinctive thing the school does. It looks like exactly what this brief asked for.

**Why it fails:** the **newsletter test.** There is no human doing anything in that sentence. It is an announcement of a schedule, and a parent reads it the way they read a bulletin — as information about a school they already attend. It also gives a prospective parent nothing to picture, which is the one job a caption on a Reel has. Compare the same fact in Example B: *"The 7th graders are in row 3. The seniors are in row 9."* Same clock, same claim, two people in it, and the parent can now see their own kid in row 3.

# 6. Kill-test appendix

**The three tests, as applied:**

- **Newsletter test** — is this a report to people who already attend, or is there a human doing something a prospective parent can picture their own child inside?

## Rewrites forced by the tests

### Rewrite 1 — Example C caption
Documented in §3 with the failing draft. Short version: three adjectives were doing the photograph's job.

### Rewrite 2 — Day 18 ("Table")

**Draft, rejected:**
> *"[12:20]. [Table 6]. [ADULT NAME] eats here most days. Because belonging isn't a program — it's who's sitting with you."*

**Why it died:** the last clause is a **moral voiceover** and it is a **new tagline** in everything but font. It also fails the any-school test cleanly: every school in the set would put "belonging isn't a program" on a wall tomorrow. And it converts a fact into a promise — a parent worried about bullying reads "belonging isn't a program" as marketing and reads "there are four seats open at it right now" as a photograph they are looking at.

**Shipped version** (in §2C): the adult, the year, and the four open seats. Nothing else. **The restraint is the argument.**

### Rewrite 3 — Day 22 ("Jump") closing card

**Draft, rejected:** `From 8th to 9th, without changing buildings — or losing who you are.`
**Why it died:** adjective-free but slogan-shaped, and the second clause is a promise nobody can keep or measure.
**Shipped version:** `Same building. Same [10:05]. [Nine] months apart.` Three facts. The parent supplies the meaning, which is the only way they will believe it.

## The standing check, for whoever posts

Before anything goes up, in this order:

1. **Is there a person in it doing something, and do we know their name and grade?** If no → do not post.
2. **Does one distinctive OLS thing appear?** Lake/brick/chapel *with people* · the crest small and in-world · both divisions at [10:05] · marked student work · a named adult. If none → do not post.
3. **Swap the logo.** Still works? → do not post.
4. **Strip the adjectives.** Nothing left? → do not post.
5. **Is there an adjective in the first five seconds?** → recut.
6. **Is this a belonging post?** Then: no campaign question, no resolution line, no sticker, no CTA.
7. **Did we voiceover the moral?** Cut the last line. It is almost always the last line.
8. **Is the horizon line in this post?** Then it must be Day 3, 21 or 30, and nowhere else.
